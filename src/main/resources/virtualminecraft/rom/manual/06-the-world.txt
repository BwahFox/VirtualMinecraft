The world outside

This is the part no other computer has: it can see the world it is standing in, and touch it.

Put a Bus Cable next to the computer and run it wherever you like. Anything on that cable -- a monitor, a disk drive, a modem -- belongs to the computer. Blocks touching the computer count too, so a short setup needs no cable at all.

Redstone. The computer reads the signal on each of its six sides and can drive one. rs on its own lists all six. rs set right 7 puts a level of 7 out to the right. rs wake 8 makes a sleeping machine start up when the signal reaches 8, which is how you wire a computer to a lever.

Containers. A chest on the bus is readable and writable: inv lists them, inv 1 lists what is in the first, inv move 1 3 2 pushes a stack from one into another. The Inventory app is the same thing with a picture.

The world itself. world prints where the machine is, the day and time, the weather, the biome and who is standing near. The World app shows it live, with a strip for the time of day.

Sound. The computer has a speaker of its own and makes noise for the world to hear -- Music writes songs on it, and any program can.

Chat. It can read the chat and say things in it, which is how a machine tells you something while you are three hundred blocks away.
