The parts, and the case

Right-click the case and it opens: four slots along the top -- RAM, CPU, GFX, HDD -- your own inventory below, and under the slots a line saying what the machine adds up to right now.

Memory and a processor are the minimum. Without them nothing happens at all, and the screen says which one is missing rather than sitting there looking broken.

A graphics card gives it a picture. Without one the machine runs perfectly well; it simply has nowhere to draw, and a monitor wired to it says so.

A hard drive gives it a /disk of its own. Without one it still boots -- from the ROM, which is built in and read-only -- and a floppy or a CD is then your only place to keep things.

Parts come in I, II and III. The case does not decide what you get; it decides how far you may go. A Memory III in a Basic Computer gives you the Basic Computer's ceiling, not its own -- it is not refused, it is just capped, and it still works. So a bigger case is worth having when your parts have outgrown the one you started in, and not before.

Hover any slot and it tells you the ceiling before you commit.

There are three cases. A Basic Computer is small and boots straight to the shell -- no desktop, no icons, the prompt filling the screen. A Computer is the middle one and the one most things are written for. An Advanced Computer is the big one: more memory, a bigger picture, more of a processor core to itself.
